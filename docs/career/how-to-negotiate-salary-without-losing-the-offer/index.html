How do you negotiate your salary without losing the offer?

VVViral VeerUpdated 2026-10-12From experience, labelled as such
Short answer

Negotiate after the offer, not during the interview. Ask once, with a number and a reason, in a friendly tone. Offers almost never get pulled for a polite ask. They get pulled for ultimatums, lies and repeated haggling.

The 6-step method

  1. Wait for the written offer.Before the offer you have no leverage. After it, the company has already decided it wants you and spent money getting there.
  2. Say thank you first, honestly.Enthusiasm is leverage. A hiring manager pushes for a candidate who clearly wants to join.
  3. Ask with a number, not a feeling.'I was hoping for something closer to 18 lakh' beats 'is there any flexibility?'. Vague asks get vague answers.
  4. Give one clean reason.A competing offer, the market band for the role, or a specific skill you bring. One reason. Not a list of expenses.
  5. Ask about the whole package.Joining bonus, variable pay, notice-period buyout and review timing are often easier to move than base.
  6. Ask once, then decide.One round, maybe two. People who come back a fourth time look like they will be a difficult employee.

VVBut here's the important part

I have been the person on the other side of the table for a long time. Here is the thing nobody tells candidates: the hiring manager usually wants you to negotiate a little. It confirms their judgment that they picked someone who knows their value.

What I have seen get offers pulled is not the ask. It is the fake competing offer that gets checked, the 'I need an answer in 24 hours' ultimatum, and the candidate who agreed, then came back for more after the paperwork started.

Negotiate like someone who will be working with these people next month. Because you will.

Common mistakes people make

  • Negotiating in the first interview. You are not an offer yet. You are a risk.
  • Anchoring on your current salary. The company does not care what you earn now. It cares about the band for this role and the alternatives it has.
  • Using personal expenses as the reason. EMIs and school fees are real, but they are your problem, not a reason the company should pay more.
  • Bluffing. HR teams in India talk to each other more than you think.
  • Winning the negotiation and losing the relationship. A hard-won extra lakh is not worth a manager who starts you on the back foot.

Frequently asked questions

Is it rude to negotiate salary in India?

No. It is expected at mid and senior levels, and increasingly at entry level. Tone matters more than the act.

How much more should I ask for?

Ten to twenty percent above the offer is a normal ask if you have a reason. Beyond that you need a competing offer or a rare skill.

Should I tell them my current CTC?

You can decline politely. Say you would rather discuss the band for this role. If they insist, give the full number including variable, and move on.

Can an offer really be withdrawn for negotiating?

Rarely, and almost always because of how it was done, not that it was done.
