How will AI change jobs?

Viral VeerUpdated 2026-10-12From experience, labelled as such
Short answer

AI will not replace most jobs this decade. It will replace the parts of jobs that are repeatable, written and checkable. The people at risk are not in any one profession. They are the people who do only the repeatable part of their profession.

What actually changes

  1. Tasks go before jobs.Drafting, summarising, first-pass analysis, routine code and customer replies are already being handed to AI. The job remains. Its shape changes.
  2. Junior work gets squeezed.The entry-level tasks that trained people for twenty years are the easiest to automate. Companies will have to rethink how juniors learn.
  3. Judgment becomes the premium.Knowing which answer is right, which client to call, and which risk to take. AI gives you options. Someone still has to choose.
  4. Generalists with tools beat specialists without them.A marketer who can use AI for analysis outperforms a pure analyst who refuses to.
  5. New roles appear quietly.Nobody announces them. Someone in a team starts doing it, and two years later it has a title.

But here's the important part

I have run technology teams through several waves: outsourcing, cloud, mobile. Every wave had the same two groups. People who learned the new tool early and became more valuable, and people who waited for it to blow over.

This wave is faster and wider, but the pattern holds. The question is not 'will AI take my job?'. It is 'will someone using AI take my job?'. That is a question you can actually act on.

Three moves this year: use AI daily for real work, not demos. Get better at the human parts, which means judgment, relationships and taste. And move closer to where money is made in your company, because those roles are protected last.

Common mistakes people make

  • Waiting for the hype to settle. It will not, and the skill gap compounds.
  • Learning AI as theory. Courses on machine learning are not the same as using the tools in your actual job.
  • Assuming your profession is safe because it needs a licence. Licences protect the signature, not the hours of work behind it.
  • Panicking. Fear makes people freeze. Curiosity makes them useful.

Frequently asked questions

Which jobs are most vulnerable to AI?

Roles built mostly on producing text, summaries, routine code, basic analysis or scripted customer support. The vulnerability is in the task mix, not the title.

Will AI replace programmers?

It will replace a lot of programming. Programmers who understand systems, users and trade-offs will be more valuable, not less.

What should students learn in the AI era?

Fundamentals of their field, how to use AI tools fluently, and how to communicate and persuade. The last one is the most under-taught.

Should a non-technical person learn AI?

Yes, as a user, not a builder. Learn to ask good questions, verify answers and build small workflows. That is enough to be ahead of most colleagues.
